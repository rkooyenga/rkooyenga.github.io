Yesterday, or a lifetime ago—one of a few now—I was forced to start life over. Not like a career change, I mean *over*. A reset of everything: friends, family, decades in business, my woman of near a decade, bank accounts, house, cars, my suits, shirts, boxers, and 1000 CDs, Italian couch, and weird folded glass table. Everything. And I had to do this because one day someone is saying I have to see some *Wolf of Wall Street* movie as its character is exactly me (minus the drugs and helicopter), and shortly thereafter, someone said something about me to some people, and the pen is truly mighty. What can I say?

"Unusual obstacles," I've said a couple of times, still not sure the best way to summarize and skip past it. A hell of a lot of what I’d built was taken from me. Vindication came a bit late—better than never, I suppose—but the cash value isn't impressive. What's done is done, and most parties apologized by now. But I decided to move on, escape the tornado by walking away from the circle of people who destroyed my life. It took a long, long time to claw my way back to anything resembling stability.

Now, I’ve finally reached a point where I can breathe again, start planning. Without getting into all the details, I was in a business situation that—let’s be blunt—amounted to embezzlement, but it came wrapped in character assassination. There were false, very serious allegations made against me, and against the woman who most just called my wife (though not officially, as I had not enough respect for churches and government to ask their blessing. And Prince just said something once I thought was cool). The accusations were baseless, absurd, and certainly suboptimal in their timing. None of that has any bearing on how effective they were at damaging me.
Despite my track record, and the fact that in every single case I had been the leader—on the front lines, in the trenches—those people who looked up to me suddenly looked down. Men are curious in this way. Not to digress, but I bring it up in another recent article, talking about rules for men in leadership roles, and suggested *Mutiny on the Bounty* as a way for women, for instance, to get a sense of something they don't consider or necessarily know is a thing. Men, when they sense weakness or are led to believe someone has fallen or isn't who they thought, consciously or not, will mutiny. They will eat you alive, actually.

In my case, a convergence of existing doubt, a misunderstanding, and some paranoia added up to a suspicion, or a conviction, that I and my girl had done something crazy.

And so only two conclusions were really possible:

1.  It sounds crazy because it is crazy—it never happened. (Please pick this one, please!)
2.  Or: "Ray has done some things that don’t make sense... that’s not the Ray we know." (Oh, thank God.) "Maybe he snapped, had some kind of trauma or breakdown." (God. Fucking. Dammit. Facepalm).

So close, guys—you almost had it. But before it’s all over, oh, I *will* go insane. Trust me on that.

There's sex, drugs, and international intrigue: Mexican cartel, some felonies, embezzling, including a stolen Ford—which, anyone who knows me, right there is a problem. Three RX-7s, a Nismo Z, a G Coupe, a 4-series coupe… I'm a 2-seater sports car guy who dreams of Adam Saruwatari's 3-rotor RX-7. I don't want a fucking Ford or American-made Civic garbage.

The hardest part wasn’t losing the businesses I built. It was losing the ones I was building. Would've been a game-changer. Not the cash flow of the moment—that paled in comparison to what I was building, and in some cases had already built.
One of our main projects was potentially being sold for close to a million. I don’t even factor that in too much, since I still don’t know what happened to it in the end. But I had high confidence in a short-term deal, and other opportunities with real financing potential. And I would’ve done more to prove I was in it, all in, if only my partner had been able to see—really see—what I had.
But the part that killed me—the part that sealed our fate—was the challenge of reputation damage.

I didn’t even realize it at the time. I couldn't figure out why people suddenly couldn't act normal, keep their word, honor deals. A year later, it became clear. Well no, that's not quite fair; it was *bragged about*—that's the moment I knew. There's a story I published in three places about that night.
It was admitted to—laughed about. My partner had tanked these deals. Without that extra effort, just the initial go-around or seed-planting was enough to destroy me and make me feel like I was going nuts.
The rumors had spread and stuck, permeating my circle long after the facts no longer mattered. This would go on for a decade and, to a less damaging degree, persists now with one or two people, and the attempt to increase that number is still happening to my knowledge, so I've been told.

A challenge that makes it especially bad: in my world, friends are partners and employees 90% of the time—and even girlfriends had been co-workers or collaborators. I found myself having to defend, for years, against things that never happened—while evidence existed in abundance to prove they didn’t. Add to that the 10 years some of these people had worked with me, in the pit, on the product I spec’d, on the most visible components I built—and yet I still had to give up.

I should’ve walked away years earlier. But it’s not hard to see why someone would resist that—especially when the people feeding you advice are themselves believing utter nonsense and can’t even follow basic logic. And even the one person who knew the truth—who was there through all of it—left me. Her reason? “Your friends and partner did this to us.” She also said: "Just walk away."

Walk away from what? From who I am? From a decade of building? From the relationships? From the people who told me I was their mentor, their inspiration? Just go work at Costco? I mean, I’m an entrepreneur with no degree, no capital, no connections outside that goddamn circle. I’ve always run my own thing. Do I put myself down as a reference on my blank résumé?

And for what—because someone said ridiculous things about me? And even though I had a totally undeserved reputation as a hustler, a sales genius—“hustler extraordinaire,” as one girlfriend put it—I couldn't just say, “That never happened, get over it”? So yeah, admitting defeat, conceding to that absurd narrative, and clocking in to mop floors felt like a radical suggestion. "Hey kid, you just went from nothing to really making it and everyone is proud. What are you going to do next?" "Well Julie, I was thinking that toilet seat over there needs some fucking scrubbing, if you'll excuse me."

Should I wear a Hugo suit or the Louis? I've got like 30k of them, you choose. Oh, that's right, those are gone too. Everything is. And getting them back, if I ever wanted to? Minimum wage is probably not the most direct path, right?

But wild as it sounds—it would’ve worked out in the end, this starting over. I learned that the hard way. I would've ascended in any organization, large or small, so long as that poison circle didn't follow me. I did eventually do it, just too late. And I don’t think there are any words anyone could’ve said to convince me to do it sooner. When things got mortally dangerous? Sure, I was open to change then.

A couple of years earlier? Maybe. But it’s a wild suggestion. And no, I didn’t end up at Costco—but I didn’t land far from there either. Actually, Costco would’ve been easier and paid better than what I had to do. But over time, I leveled up. Got better jobs. Eventually, I had a real career. I earned respect. I held real responsibility.

Now, maybe once a year, someone calls and brings up that old situation, and I just think: “What? You’re *still* on that?” It never happened. I don’t even think about it anymore. They think I’m crazy for giving up having companies or money to be a wage-earner. Well—guys—*you did this*. I fought it as hard as I could and was criticized even for that. So I gave up my whole life and who I am for you. You told me to, in so many words, and even forced it by excluding me during the height of the paranoia.

This position I’m in? Perhaps not flattering compared to who you knew me as. But if you knew what the position is or things like has anywhere there ever had it or is it maybe somehow made for you? There's a lot of unknown things there as  I never wanted my life to have to touch the past. It was years before I dared update my facebook. But *you* destroyed that with your sewing circle. It cost me everything. I had no options left. I got tired of defending myself. So I chose a life with normal people—people who aren’t crazy. People who don’t doubt my value. People who aren’t obsessed with paranoia or power plays.

And what’s wild? In my new life, no one doubts me. No one accuses me of anything, or would ever dream of it. No one fires me for paranoid reasons, then hits the phone to smear me to my exes, and a year later is calling behind my back to sabotage what I spent a year building, only to make themselves $2k when they could've made $200k that year for doing nothing but being an asshole quietly. No calls to my exes to smear me in my personal life too. Because that’s not normal behavior!!! But in my old circle, it was so normal it was barely worthy of mention. Would I like to pay myself a $15k check this week? Sure. But what would it cost me? I had diseases in my 20s – life-changing conditions. When I stepped back from *them*, I got better. Something external to me was so toxic it was literally killing me.

Someone heard me wrap up a business call recently and said: “I get it now. Why you're doing this. You seem happier; you like doing this.” Yeah, I do. I get to talk to business owners or ops managers, learn about their challenges, and figure out what I can do to help. I can be creative and be *me*, frankly. And I get more of that freedom and am encouraged to be that way. It's great, in a sense. And I can pivot to any department I want. I don't write any paychecks, and I get mine on direct deposit, so I can take off on Fridays if I need to.

Yeah—I’m finally free of the FUD. I'm happy people have been coming around, but Christ, I'm not *that* happy it took years for them to believe what I said from day one. But nothing I could say would ever change some people’s minds. And when someone holds power over you, believes lies, and won’t accept truth—you are in a situation that will kill you. You have to leave.

So I did. I left the circle I literally built. It was as stupid and unnecessarily uphill as I feared, for longer than I pictured. But three companies along the way made something I never used to doubt clear: that those who did doubt me, and entertained for a second the crazy narrative that got out of control, were not just a minority, but normal people would never have even believed any of it. Every billion-dollar company I've worked for has made accommodations and offers to get me to stay, regardless of this initial agreement or reasons for leaving, which varies. Nobody has been anxious to end things prematurely. That's notable.

I joined the real world. And it welcomed me with open arms. Glancing in the rearview, there's some cupped-ear whispering I can't quite make out. It's more funny than sad, I think.
