When six or seven-figure revenue drops to *literal* zero, what once felt impossible becomes hard to deny. A chilling thought.

Does it scare you? Me either—why would it? Who would *ever*? It wasn’t on my mind at all. Odds like 1 in 10,000 feel safe—until you’re the one.

So—what's your life like? What do you have that you truly value?

Now imagine everything you’ve worked for—really worked for—just *gone*. You’re living the dream, or right on the cusp… and then it all disappears. The safe’s empty. Nobody returns your calls—unless it’s to ask why you did it.

> “Did what?”

You don’t even know yet. You’re fired. Your wife’s gone—or will be soon, once she sees you fail to fix this. Timmy’s unwelcome at the academy next semester.

> “But I didn’t do anything!”

Now you sound defensive, and most people take that as proof of guilt.

Could that really happen?

I’ll tell you what happened to me.

I was grinding—three projects at once. My girlfriend was screening candidates for help. I hit a wall with a phone system issue, so we took a nap. Power nap. We woke up to some wild accusations—like, way out there. But the person was *dead serious*.

This was the same person I’d just flown across the country to meet. We were discussing the sale of my business, the future of all our ventures, funding. Serious stakes. I had nothing in my pocket—everything was tied up in the projects—and now suddenly lawyers and “culpability” were being thrown around. 

Then the phone started ringing.

Three people. Three countries. All asking: *“What the hell is going on?”*

It’d be funny. Like a comedy of errors. Except there’s no one left to laugh with.

Emails flying. People I’d worked with for six, ten years. One of them told me I was his mentor. I liked all of them. And I was watching them evaporate.

One panicked, all panicked. Bad time for a nap, in retrospect.

I sent a bulk email, fast—something like: “Hey, I don’t know what this is, but if we’re going to hit deadlines, it needs to stop.”

Most of them never replied again. That was the last communication I had with several people I built entire businesses with. Every one of those projects died.

---

It’s only been five minutes, and maybe you're already thinking this is insane.

Brother, you're Harrison Ford shouting about a one-armed man.

You try not to sound paranoid. You downplay it. You lean on your network. You think: *They know me.* You underestimate how easily people believe lies—especially the ones who never had much faith in the first place.

Six months later: bankrupt. Nothing worked. Your circle’s gone. You’re pawning gear and sleeping at a Motel 6. Cockroaches optional. Sierra the lot lizard’s not so bad in the right lighting.

Sorry about the wife.

---

You know what a sucker punch is, right? So you understand: it's possible to lose a fight before you even know you're in one.

You're in your bathrobe, and you just got mugged by a magician.

Your wallet’s gone. Your life’s gone. Replaced by a nightmare.

And I don’t have to imagine it. I *lived* it.

It’s actually worse than I’m making it sound.

My case is rare—edge-case weird—but in a world of copycat content creators and SEO-choked algorithms, rare cases get ignored. People don’t want nuance. They want categories. And if you don’t fit one?

> Still.recommends: nothing.

I don’t have a kid named Timmy. But I might’ve wanted one. That choice was taken too.

---

The good news: there *is* a way out.

Even better? A lot of the things that could prevent this—*you’re probably already doing some of them.*

The bad news?

If you love your privacy, get ready to stretch. You're going to need to expose yourself—strategically. That risk appetite? You’ll have to calibrate it. For me, if I could go back?

> I’d give anything to prevent what happened. Because it cost me *everything.*

Your network’s going to change. Your circle. Some people gotta go. And you’ve got to *replace* them. Loyalty matters—but it’s dangerous if you’re not measuring IQ, integrity, and pattern recognition alongside it.

Sounds simple. It isn’t.

You’ll be lucky to count your real friends on one hand. But here’s the thing: if this happens to you, be prepared to lose *all of them.*

---

Is it really that severe?

I get it—you’re not walking around with an anti-lightning-strike helmet.

Odds, right?

But that stab in the back? That came from *my* most trusted partner. My closest male friend outside my dad. It was *inexplicable* for years.

Now, I see it clearly.

Were there signs? Absolutely. I always knew, deep down. But I got comfortable. I forgot. Ten years is a long time to feel safe.

Too long.
