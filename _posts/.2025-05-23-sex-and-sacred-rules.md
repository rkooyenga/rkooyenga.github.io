# THE SACRED RULE: ON SEX, LOYALTY, AND THE COST OF BREAKING BONDS

They tell you it’s not a big deal. That cheating is natural. That monogamy is outdated. But they’re wrong—and worse, they know it. Every person knows, in their gut, even before the rules are spelled out, that this is betrayal. It hurts. It scars. It destroys something sacred. That’s why nearly every civilization, religion, tribe, or clan throughout time treated it as taboo. Why? Because it matters.

The same way a child knows a pedophile touched them and that something irreversible just happened, a betrayed lover knows that part of them was just torn away. Sex isn’t a handshake. It’s a chemical, spiritual, and social covenant. And when that covenant is broken, it rewires everything—who you trust, what you believe, and how you see the world. 

We used to punish betrayal. We used to call it what it was. Today? We throw confetti over it, stream it, celebrate it with songs and TikToks. Imagine someone sneaking food from starving prisoners in a concentration camp, and we call it a “relationship issue.” We’ve let idiocy become ideology. We've made excuses for behavior that used to get people exiled or executed. 

Let me make it clear: I’ve been on every side of this. I’ve slept with the “church girls” and the undercover freaks. I’ve been cheated on by someone who was later caught in gangbangs with men who, by disturbing coincidence, included someone who tried to rape me as a child. What are the odds? I’ve spent years celibate, on purpose, training my will like a monk, burning with invisible fire from resisting temptation. I’ve breathed through the madness of restraint, and I’ve seen what happens when you give in, and it’s for nothing.

You go three, five, ten years without sex, finally meet someone who connects with your soul, and when you give yourself to them—really give—it’s not just a good night. It’s a bond. Something cosmic happens. You know it, even if they don’t. That’s why betrayal cuts so deep—because you can’t go back. You can’t unknow it. That’s why people break.

Some people will never understand what they’re missing because they’ve never had it. I know people who have never been in love—not real love—and it shows. They mock loyalty, preach polyamory, redefine “commitment,” and post memes about being “free.” But they’re not free. They’re numb. They’re lost. And some are starting to know it.

This isn’t just religion talking. It’s not just me talking. Every major world religion—Christianity, Islam, Judaism—despite their vast differences, all say the same thing here: sex has gravity. Loyalty is sacred. Adultery is sin. And it’s echoed by science. Oxytocin, vasopressin, microchimerism, emotional imprinting—it’s all there. Even the law still reflects it in divorce courts and child custody rulings.

We live in a world that wants to normalize dysfunction because it’s easier than facing the truth: that most of our misery is self-inflicted. That we've handed the mic to morons and let them dictate the rules. That we let social decay masquerade as “freedom.” This isn’t conservative. It isn’t liberal. It’s human. It’s what works. Loyalty works. Trust works. Real love works.

The rest is a slow suicide of civilization. You don’t have to buy a Bible to know that. Just listen to your gut. Just look at the wreckage.

Because I promise you—I’ve lived it—I’m not wrong.
