Jump in blind, no research, no feel for the market, no adjusted plan? Just a sweaty-haired slob with a gin-flushed face rolling those dice? It is an option yes. Seriously, don't do it, lol. I didnt expect this to be to pthis run and still wouldn't but that's not the point. The point is you need targets that data supports then a plan for what to do if hit. Data says 130 is 90% Ok so what are you doing then sitting on it waiting for it to drop back down? Recoup principle + 30% profit? That's a plan. Do you have stops placed? Just don't gamble. When gamblers win it's luck. I've never had luck just hard work.

I was cataloguing for tracking on a future blog like this one which didn't exist yet and the raykooyenga.com which somehow still doesn't. Went in depth with a couple close people. This is actually funny one of them ended up staying here and was feet away a action I'm not accustomed to having an audience for besides a girlfriends over the years. But I always picture how nutty everythign must look without explanation. But when something outside of whats expected movment happens you have to be prepared to alter course which is the intense part as it's a big interruption and high stakes moment. I'm an anti-gambler like I don't enjoy risk even a little. So, observe Ray in turbo autistic mode until the sanity of a plan calms the waters.

1st thing to happen is determining if pivot is necessary. If so, cue Danny Glover "I'm gettin too old for this shit" imagine a desk covered in books and notes and someone just clears it off with their arm saying all that can wait. That's the start and I'm typing and reading like I'm fighting time, which I literally am. And 1 to 3 days later I've got new tooling and a picture and decisions made and then it's time to basically sit and let it play out, resume those other things strewn across the floor. 

Looked chaotic but think of the stakes, I'm living solely on investments and to not take moves seriously is to be ok with death. Because that's on the table. Life vs death, failure is that in a literal sense. Sounds extreme yes. I've been told that many times genreally by people who starve if I fail. That's if anything the secret to startups. That's the psychology of grit. The reason I have no failed launches, and why 100's had a job.

In previous runs we'd post our moves on X but I hadn't been very active there and dropped out of crypto twitter long ago so I don't have the same audience of crypto people or even size, my is audience 1/10th what it was before. Broad strokes are on Twitter/X, though. I talked about targets. I didn't want to speculate $120K; rather, I wanted to see where things went, but somewhere $110K to $120K was a tentative exit in the Winter forecast. Meaning I should be on the train and selling right now unless the plan says new information indicates a pivot is warranted. Which is likely as it wasn't that optimistic of a goal. Conservative actually which is the point though, selling first not last. And I couldn't comfortably and confidently see north of $125K or $130K that far back; for price discovery, you have to be watching.

You won't hit the exact bottom or top. You can be the first out that fire escape, even if everyone looks at you funny. That's often a good sign in my experience. You aren't running in fear or concerned with the crowd; you're following a step in your plan that outlined doing 'x' in consideration of 'y' and competing with the crowd. That's what matters. 

Don't risk death seeing how close you can get your neck to the sword. If you're a foot away and hit your targets, just walk away.

Buy back in for me was at the highest, 80-90 range atleast on BTC and I'm mostly very liquid cash and stables. I moved out at 101 I think but did a 50% buyback prematurely and that's gonna hurt I setup some alerts and automation to help, new APIs, looking for signals on a couple of key accounts' socials, sentiment checks, historical comparisons, and very exciting, action-packed data for the heart of it (the election) through March. That was fun, actually. Too much at once given all the app projects. Then news happens, direction changes, and I spend two days writing custom scripts pausing everythings else without hesitation as this is instant funding for eveything else right? And planning on 1 to 15 days, but in mere hours you get your answer, exciting. 

In the end my head's a mess. Something really shook me this month; such that it changes everything or nothing, I don't know. I just stopped caring about almost anything. Like a fuse blew. So, yes, I abandoned my own plan at end and everything else I was excited for. None of what I built for analysis and alerting, is even running. Hell, I don't remember the apps I was releasing the day the fuse blew; that's what I was doing, a promo video. for a couple things and I went blank 3 weeks later still dont know or care. Lucky I remember my name. 

Rough guess probably out $15K, minimum $10K, not generational wealth exactly but enough to matter a lot. And the cost of not paying attention. What's weird I don't "feel" the regret I should. No FOMO. Not really remorse. I feel blank. Like it's very bad. I know. But there's a perspective here timeline has cahnged on lots of things look at my age. The end goal is gone.
